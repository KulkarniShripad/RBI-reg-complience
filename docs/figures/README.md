# Figures for the report and the journal

All figures are SVG (vector, so they scale to any column width without blurring). They are generated by `urcc-ef-backend/scripts/figures/make_figures.js`:

```bash
cd urcc-ef-backend
npm run figures          # rewrite every figure from eval/results/*.json
npm run eval:journal     # rerun all evaluations, then the figures
```

**Style.**
- Architecture figures (1–7) are black and white.
- Evaluation figures (8–20) are grayscale. Series are told apart by shade, hatching and marker shape, so they survive black-and-white printing and colour-blind reading.
- Figure titles are not drawn inside the images; use the captions below.
- Every number in Figs. 8–20 is read from the evaluation outputs, so rerunning an evaluation (for example with the Gemini judge) and then `npm run figures` updates them.

To insert them:
- **LaTeX:** convert with `rsvg-convert -f pdf figXX.svg -o figXX.pdf` or `inkscape figXX.svg --export-type=pdf`, then use `\includegraphics`.
- **Word:** insert the SVG directly.

## Architecture

**Fig. 1 — `fig01_system_architecture.svg`.**
*Architecture of URCC-EF.* RBI directions and bank evidence are processed into a shared SQLite knowledge base. Comprehension serves questions from it. For compliance, each (rule, evidence) pair is routed by the adaptive reasoning-modality router to the rule engine, the graph evaluator, retrieval-grounded LLM judgment or human review. Every decision is one of five labels and carries an audit trail.

**Fig. 2 — `fig02_rule_extraction_pipeline.svg`.**
*Rule extraction pipeline.* Seven deterministic stages turn RBI PDFs into clauses, rule atoms (variable, operator, threshold, unit, base), definitions, resolved cross-references and clause-aligned chunks, indexed for keyword and semantic search. Rule typing (A–G, risk, version in force) is applied on read. Quality is measured against a 62-rule gold set.

**Fig. 3 — `fig03_comprehension_subsystem.svg`.**
*Regulatory comprehension.* A question is analysed (category, abbreviations, follow-up rewriting), searched by keyword and by meaning over clause-aligned chunks, fused by reciprocal rank, and answered from the full text of the retrieved clauses with numbered citations. There is an extractive fallback when no LLM is available. A source viewer and a meaning-guarded plain-language restatement support the reader.

**Fig. 4 — `fig04_compliance_checking_subsystem.svg`.**
*Compliance checking.*
- (A) Automatic check from a bank's public document: parse, detect the profile, extract and review figures, then apply anchored rules, discovered rules, the disclosure checklist and qualitative matching.
- (B) Bank Explorer: stored bank data checked through the adaptive router.

Both produce the five decisions with citations and recommended actions.

**Fig. 5 — `fig05_adaptive_router.svg`.**
*Adaptive reasoning-modality router (Algorithm 1).* Two evidence gates and a hard override (applicable rule engine, complete and uncontradicted evidence) come before a cost-aware argmin over modalities. The argmin uses a learned error model P̂(error | m, f), and its weights are tuned on the validation split under an accuracy constraint τ.

**Fig. 6 — `fig06_graph_subsystem.svg`.**
*Network (graph) compliance subsystem.* Entities, typed edges, exposures and capital are validated in the graph store. The graph engine derives connected-counterparty groups, related-party paths and the bank's own group. The evaluator compares aggregate exposures with 53 network rules resolved to their RBI paragraphs.

**Fig. 7 — `fig07_benchmark_and_evaluation_protocol.svg`.**
*Benchmark construction and evaluation protocol.* 1,393 cases are generated over 215 real rules and nine case types and split by rule into train / validation / test. Error models are fitted on train, all choices are made on validation, and every system is evaluated once on test.

## Compliance results (test split, n = 294)

**Fig. 10 — `fig10_system_accuracy.svg`.**
*Accuracy of baselines and routers.*
- Automated accuracy counts escalations as undecided.
- End-to-end accuracy counts escalations as resolved by a reviewer, with the reviewer's cost charged.
- The adaptive router R4 reaches 92.2% / 96.9%, against 83.3% / 92.9% for the earlier heuristic router R0 and 72.5% for RAG + LLM alone.

**Fig. 11 — `fig11_accuracy_cost_frontier.svg`.**
*Accuracy–cost trade-off.* End-to-end accuracy against cost per case (log scale) for every system and all 72 tuned R4 configurations. The R4 operating points lie on the Pareto frontier. R4 (τ) is more accurate than R0 at lower cost.

**Fig. 12 — `fig12_accuracy_by_case_type.svg`.**
*Automated accuracy by case type.* R4 is at 100% on every case type except ambiguous (80%) and adversarial (56%). Adversarial statements of intention remain hard for every system.

**Fig. 13 — `fig13_accuracy_by_category.svg`.**
*Automated accuracy by requirement category.* Deterministic categories (C, D, G) are solved by the rule engine and graph evaluator. R4's largest gain over the fixed hybrid is on Boolean requirements (B, +33 points), followed by qualitative (E, +8) and quantitative (A, +7) requirements.

**Fig. 14 — `fig14_ablations.svg`.**
*Ablation study.* Removing the rule engine or evidence grounding costs 20–35 points. Among router components, the learned error model contributes most (−7.8 points automated). The hard override leaves accuracy unchanged on this benchmark; it is kept as a correctness guarantee.

**Fig. 15 — `fig15_significance.svg`.**
*Significance of the improvements.* Accuracy differences with 95% cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals (resampled by rule) and exact McNemar p-values. All comparisons of R4 are significant. The fully automated operating point is not significantly better than a static rule-first policy (p = 0.22).

**Fig. 16 — `fig16_confusion_matrices.svg`.**
*Decision confusion matrices* for (a) R4 and (b) R0. Rows are expected decisions, columns produced decisions, and an escalation counts as Human review.
- R4 never confuses compliant with non-compliant.
- Most of its residual disagreements are escalations of non-compliant cases to review, or Ambiguous vs Human review (which the benchmark accepts as interchangeable for partial-implementation cases).

**Fig. 17 — `fig17_route_mix.svg`.**
*Where R4 sends each requirement category.* Deterministic engines handle C, G and most of A and D. RAG + LLM handles qualitative and relational requirements, with human review reserved for thin or conflicting evidence.

## Extraction and comprehension results

**Fig. 18 — `fig18_extraction_recall.svg`.**
*Rule extraction against the hand-verified gold set.* Exact recall (operator and value) rose from 45.2% to 85.5% overall and from 16.7% to 83.3% on network limits after the extraction fixes. Operator accuracy is 100%.

**Fig. 19 — `fig19_retrieval_chunking.svg`.**
*Clause-aligned vs fixed-window retrieval.* Top-5 recall and MRR@10 on (a) 79 questions generated from gold rules and (b) 36 hand-written questions. Clause-aligned hybrid retrieval roughly doubles MRR@10 on rule-seeking questions (0.64 vs 0.31).

**Fig. 20 — `fig20_simplification.svg`.**
*Plain-language restatement.* Flesch–Kincaid grade before and after the rule-based simplifier. The meaning guard keeps every rule intact (100% identical re-extraction), but readability gains are small. Larger gains require the guarded LLM rewrite.
